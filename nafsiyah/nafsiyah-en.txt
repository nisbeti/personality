Building and developing the Islamic psychology

First: Defining concepts
There are several concepts that must be defined before starting the topic of building and developing the psychology, which are:
- Personality
- Mentality
- Psychology
- Intellectual foundation
- Intellectual leadership
- Ideas and concepts
1- The concept of personality
The term personality is an artificial source, derived from the word (person), and it refers to any body that has height and appearance(1). Its usage has predominated for humans over others, without preventing its use and derivatives in non-human contexts, as it is applied to something that has prominence, manifestation, and distinction from its surroundings.
Philosophers have used it to denote the conscious self of its being, independent in its will. Perhaps in this usage, there is a transition from the material usage and the material description of a human in relation to its environment to the moral usage and moral description, in that what most distinguishes a human from its surroundings and from others is its mental and psychological reality. Although a human is distinguished in its creation, form, and body from other tangible creatures, Allah, Exalted is He, said: “Indeed, We have created man in the best stature”(2); yet its moral distinction, that is, its mental and psychological distinction from other tangible beings, is greater and more significant.

The use of the term shifted to denote this distinction and became a convention, thus it became a term that refers to a person in terms of their intellectual aspect regarding thinking and awareness, and their psychological aspect regarding feelings, inclinations, and behavioral motivations. Accordingly, the personality of every individual consists of two components, with no third: the intellectual and the psychological. The person's physical appearance, whether in terms of height or width, beauty or ugliness, or attire, whether harmonious or not, has no bearing on the judgment of a person's personality; rather, the judgment focuses on the manner of their thinking and the totality of their inclinations. The term personality is thus applied as an artificial source to the individual in terms of what distinguishes them, and indeed, the greatest distinction lies in their intellect and emotions.
2- The Concept of Mentality
The term "mentality" (al-aqliya) is an artificial source derived from the word "reason" (aql), and the word reason means thinking and perception. However, making the word an artificial source adds a new meaning in addition to the meaning of reason, which is the manner of reasoning, that is, how one comprehends things. This manner does not refer to the mechanism of thinking, as that is the meaning of reason, not mentality. What is observed in humans is that in their thinking and judgment about things, they start from a conceptual framework, and the conceptual framework is a general idea that allows for the construction of subsidiary ideas upon it. Therefore, it is called a framework, even though it is an idea; but due to its generality and comprehensiveness, it is suitable for building smaller ideas upon it. Thus, relying on a conceptual framework in the process of thinking serves as a basis for judging things, that is, a foundation for reasoning about them and issuing judgments on them; this is what is meant by mentality. Therefore, mentality refers to the manner of reasoning about things, that is, adopting a conceptual framework for judging things.
The Islamic personality takes Islamic belief as the foundation for thinking and judging things. If this personality encounters an incident, it views it as fate and divine decree, and thus acts according to the patience and accountability that this perspective entails, knowing that it is ultimately good for them. On the other hand, the capitalist personality views the incident as a calamity; as if it were the end of the world for them, due to the harm they have suffered as a result of that incident. However, if they emerge unscathed from the incident and gain some benefit from it, they begin to see it as good. Thus, their measure for judging a single matter is purely utilitarian, unlike the Islamic personality, which has made Islamic belief the basis for judging the incident and determining its stance towards it.
3- The Concept of Psychology
The term "psychology" is an artificial source, derived from the word "self" (nafs). Although the word "self" has different meanings in the Arabic language, the intended meaning here relates to a person's inclinations and emotional traits. They say: "So-and-so has a good self" if he loves good for others, and they say: "He has an elevated self" if he is humble. It is noted in usage the emotional aspects of being content with the truth, or feeling arrogance, or being distinguished by eagerness and greed, or other matters that are driven by emotions and motivations.
The formulation of the artificial source from this term necessitated an increase in meaning beyond the previous meaning of the self, in accordance with the linguistic rule "every increase in structure necessitates an increase in meaning." It is a manner of connection between emotions and the intellectual framework at


[Page 6]

the person, and the intellectual foundation that transforms those feelings into regulated inclinations according to that foundation.
And a human being has a vital energy that drives him towards satisfaction, which consists of organic needs and instincts, and human behavior; that is, the actions he performs are to satisfy those organic needs and instincts. These drives for satisfaction, if they are linked to his intellectual foundation and the concepts derived from it, become inclinations and shape the psychology of the individual.
In his behavior, he is driven to satisfy an innate need or a manifestation of instincts within him, and his behavior for satisfaction is linked to a specific concept about this satisfaction. This drive to engage in behavior becomes regulated by the related concept, and in addition to that, the drive is accompanied by a certain feeling towards the behavior that satisfies, making the concept influential in the individual from two aspects:
- The intellectual aspect: that is, it determines the ruling on this satisfaction, whether it is satisfied in this way or in another, and it defines his stance towards that event and his perspective on that thing. If this occurs, he develops a mentality characterized by the concepts he holds and the intellectual foundation from which those concepts emerged.
- The psychological aspect: that is, it determines the type of feeling accompanying the satisfaction, whether it is contentment or anger, love or hatred, longing for it or aversion from it, and so on. If this occurs, the feelings accompanying the drives and the behaviors resulting from them are formulated, transforming into inclinations, and he develops a psychology characterized by the concepts that contributed to the formulation of feelings and their transformation into inclinations.
If the concepts that contribute to the formulation of feelings and their transformation into inclinations align with the concepts that determine the type of behavior, the individual acquires a distinctive personality defined by the intellectual foundation from which those controlling concepts in both the intellectual and psychological aspects emerged, thus coloring his personality. If the concepts are Islamic and stem from Islamic belief, the personality becomes Islamic; if the concepts are capitalist and stem from capitalist belief, the personality becomes capitalist. If this does not occur for a specific individual, his personality may be chaotic or contradictory.
If this happens, the belief becomes for him an intellectual leadership, meaning it provides its holder with criteria that guide his judgments about things, his drives towards them, and his inclinations towards them in a specific direction necessitated by that belief, thus becoming a leadership. And since specific ideas govern this, and those ideas stem from an intellectual foundation which is the belief, it becomes intellectual, and hence the term (intellectual leadership) arises.
- Ideas: the plural of idea, and an idea is a judgment on reality, and it is called an idea because it is the product of a thinking process, after transferring reality through the senses to the brain and linking it with previous information about that reality. The product of this linking process is a judgment on that reality, which is the idea. Thus, our statement: "There is no deity but Allah," is an idea because it is a judgment on the perceived reality from the perspective of its creation. And our statement: "Honey is sweet," is a judgment on the reality of honey after it has been transferred to the brain through the sense of taste,

And it was linked to the previous information about tastes, and the result of this thinking was the idea that honey is sweet.
- Concepts: Concepts are the meanings of ideas, and the concept is the meaning of the idea, not the meaning of its words and sentences, meaning that the idea must have a reality that a person perceives and believes in. The perception of the reality of the idea can be either through sensory perception or through mental conception. If the reality of the idea is perceived and belief in its reality is established, the idea transforms into a concept in the mind of the one who has experienced that and believed it.

For example, in saying: honey is sweet, this statement is an idea, and since the senses perceive the sweetness of honey in reality and believe this perception, meaning they affirm the correspondence of this statement to the reality it refers to, then this statement and this idea transform into a concept in the mind of the one who perceives that and believes it.
Secondly: The formation and construction of the psychology:
Since the psychology is the mixture of drives and emotions with concepts, and its regulation in a person with the concepts related to it to transform it into inclinations that are regulated in their appearance, stimulation, and desire for satisfaction according to those concepts, it is essential for the formation of the psychology to establish correct concepts about life and about things within it.
Establishing correct concepts about life and about things within it can only occur through the existence of a comprehensive idea about the universe, humanity, and life, about their relationships with what precedes them, and about their relationships with what follows them. Thus, a correct belief is necessary for the emergence of correct concepts that govern behavior and emotions.
Islamic belief is the only correct belief that provides the correct concepts about life, as it alone acknowledges the human condition of incapacity, deficiency, and natural need, and it alone convinces the mind. These are the two measures of the correctness of any belief.
In the following pages, we will discuss the role of Islamic belief in the formation and construction of the psychology, and its role in its development, as it is the only correct belief.
Formation of the Islamic psychology:
Islamic belief has come to provide humans with the correct concepts about life, and to establish the correct system that organizes their lives and behavior within it, and their positions on everything that their senses encounter from things and events. It has provided them with a set of standards to use as a basis for judging things, actions, and positions, and the concepts that blend with their emotions to transform them into inclinations.


[Page 8]

In other words, the Islamic creed has taken on the responsibility of shaping the human mind and psychology to create a distinguished personality, one that progresses in its thoughts, behaviors, and inclinations in an upward manner, ensuring the existence of that distinguished Islamic personality, safeguarding it, and guaranteeing its elevation and growth from good to better, and from high to higher.
This research will address the Islamic concepts that constitute the Islamic psychology, the concepts that ensure its preservation, and the concepts that guarantee its elevation. However, it will begin with the methods of creating the concept, that is, transforming intended ideas into concepts that influence one's judgment on things, affect one's motivations towards them, and impact the accompanying feelings of those motivations.
It has been stated in the research that an idea that affects the mentality and the psychology must transform into a concept, and it has been noted that the transformation of an idea into a concept can only occur through the sensory or conceptual understanding of the reality of the idea, along with the belief in this understanding of the reality of the idea. Sensory perception is related to tangible realities, while conceptual perception pertains to unseen realities that cannot be grasped by the senses.
The formation and construction of the psychology cannot occur all at once or in a single instance; rather, it must happen gradually, after the individual accepts the foundational creed upon which their mentality and psychology will be built. They begin to transform the ideas and information brought by the creed into concepts that act upon their judgments and inclinations, and this can be achieved in one of two ways:
1- Linking reality with information or ideas
2- Linking information and ideas with reality
The research will elaborate on each of these methods with detailed explanations and examples.
Linking reality with information:
A person lives in this universe with its:
- Things                - Events               - Relationships
They must determine their perspective towards what their senses perceive of those things, events, and relationships. They must take a stance on what occurs, regardless of their involvement in its occurrence, and they must engage in actions to satisfy their organic needs and instincts related to their surroundings. A stance must be accompanied by an inclination, in addition to the emergence of a specific motivation towards what occurs.
They will also perform actions and behaviors that correspond to what their senses perceive as a result of the stimulation of certain motivations that drive them to undertake those actions.
Naturally, for a human being, the sensation of reality generates thoughts about it, as a result of linking the perceived reality with the information they possess.
However, this is not sufficient; this linkage must align with their intellectual framework in order to achieve psychological and mental harmony; otherwise, their inclinations will contradict their concepts, leading to anxiety.


[Page 9]

When a person makes belief the foundation for thinking, that is, a basis for linking reality with information, the formation of concepts will emerge from this intellectual foundation, resulting in the shaping of motivation and its blending with the concept, thus forming the appropriate inclination for that situation or behavior.
If a person proceeds in life with this method, that is, linking everything that their senses perceive from things, events, and relationships with the information they possess based on Islamic belief, that is, on the basis of the criteria that belief provides for this type of thoughts or judgments; the person gradually walks the path of forming their psychology, step by step, just as a great building is constructed brick by brick.
Some examples of this:
1- A man sees a woman, and a woman sees a man, which stimulates the instinct of reproduction in each of them. If he moves from sensation to action without passing through the stage of thought, he degrades to the level of an animal. However, if he reflects, he will link this reality with the information he has about it, building this connection on the basis of the intellectual principle he holds, specifically with the concept related to this reality. Accordingly, he determines his position based on the concept, whether to fulfill the desire or not. If his basis for linking and measuring is Islamic belief, he reaches the ruling of prohibiting the gaze if the seen is foreign to the viewer, thus refraining from looking. By repeatedly applying this mechanism every time this reality presents itself, the stimulation of desire becomes linked to the legal ruling, which emerges from his intellectual foundation, and inclinations that support this, accompanying that process, are formed, such as: fear of Allah, fear of His punishment, aversion to sin, and longing for Paradise... thus he refrains from sin out of hope for Allah's pleasure.
2- He sees wealth and sees things that require the money he does not possess. If his intellectual basis is not Islamic belief, he will likely feel regret for not being able to obtain it, and perhaps the instinctive drive will push him to engage in wrongful behavior to acquire money and achieve satisfaction. However, if his basis for measurement is Islamic belief, he links this reality to Allah's division of sustenance, thus finding reassurance in Allah's wisdom in testing him with that trial, and he remains patient, hoping for Allah's pleasure and what Allah has prepared for His servants in Paradise.
3- If Allah has not granted him sons; whenever he sees a reality related to sons, his position will be according to his intellectual basis. He will feel regret if his foundation is not Islamic belief, but he will be content, patient, and hopeful regarding what Allah has decreed for him, believing that Allah alone grants daughters and sons, and that He makes whom He wills barren.
4- He needs money; if his measure of benefit is self-interest, he will borrow with interest, and his motivation will form with satisfaction in that. But if his measure is the legal ruling (lawful and unlawful), it will form...

The motivator has a sense of aversion and dislike for that, so he refrained from it and sought a legitimate way to save money.
Thus, every time reality presents itself to a person, and they adopt the same stance and make the same connection, the link between the motive and the concept is strengthened for them, and the inclination is shaped accordingly, at the level of each individual situation, and it extends to all the situations they encounter, leading them to develop their psychology in an upward trajectory.
In linking reality with information on a conceptual and doctrinal basis, we mention here the role of the surrounding environment in shaping a person's concepts. A good environment and a righteous community help its members form correct concepts, contribute to shaping individuals' motivations in accordance with prevailing concepts, and create in them the appropriate inclinations towards things, events, and relationships.
A corrupt environment and a corrupt society help individuals form incorrect concepts, and they shape their motivations in a way that aligns with what is prevalent in society. Thus, a contradiction arises between the intellectual foundation of individuals and the concepts, motivations, and inclinations that emerge from the influence of the surrounding environment. Therefore, you find that most people suffer from psychological disorders that lead to a constant feeling of anxiety, a lack of harmony and congruence with oneself, and dissatisfaction with oneself.
Linking Information to Reality:
A person is never free from the stages of learning they go through, and many people use the method of memorization and rote learning for the information they acquire. While this method may be suitable for a person up to a certain age and for specific subjects, it is not appropriate for them beyond that age, nor for all the things they learn. If they continue with the same method after reaching maturity, they will not become mature, because the essence of maturity is discernment. Therefore, some modern educational methods tend to adopt approaches that distance the learner from memorization and rote learning, such as experiential learning, play-based learning, self-directed learning, collaborative learning, and other methods that essentially link information to reality. This aims to equip the child or learner with this style of learning at an early stage. However, the question that must be posed here is: where is the reliance on the intellectual foundation in linking information to reality?
What is meant by linking information to reality is when learning occurs, and learning is not limited to sessions of knowledge and circles of study, but a person may hear information from a satellite channel, or hear it from someone, or read it in a pamphlet. The Islamic method in teaching requires taking information and ideas to act upon them, and this means two things:
1-     Linking information and ideas to reality
2-      The basis of this linkage is the intellectual foundation
This means forming concepts, and regarding psychology, this means the blending of motivation with the new concept, and forming the correct inclination that this concept necessitates. This also occurs gradually, not all at once, because studying the idea and linking it to its reality while relying on it...


[Page 11]

The intellectual principle requires sufficient time for the reality of the idea to be conceived and represented in a manner based on the measure that emerges from the intellectual principle.
Thus, for the formation of the psychology, the method of study and the method of learning for a Muslim must rely on understanding, and we do not mean understanding the meanings of words and sentences, but rather understanding the meaning of the idea, conceiving its reality, and believing in it, that is, recognizing its correctness through perceiving its conformity to its tangible reality, and consequently affirming that idea. This is if the idea is related to a tangible reality; however, if the idea pertains to an intangible reality, then its belief, based on the correctness of its foundation from which it emerged, creates a specific conception for it, and this conception must align with what the idea itself indicates and the affirmation of that.
So when a Muslim studies the idea that revival is intellectual elevation, he connects it to its reality. If he feels the idea's conformity to the reality it indicates and affirms that conformity, it becomes a concept for him, influencing his motives and inclinations related to that idea, such as the yearning for true revival. When he senses something in reality that corresponds to what has become a concept for him, he gains reassurance in his heart regarding the correct concept he holds.
And when he studies the legal ruling related to theft, he feels its conformity to his reality, it becomes a concept for him, and he reshapes the motives and inclinations related to that reality to align with the new concept. His psychological response to this reality becomes appropriate to the belief he holds and the measure that emerged from it, leading him to detest this act and recoil from it, remembering the punishment of Allah, Exalted is He, for those who commit it, causing him to tremble in fear of Allah and His punishment.
And when he studies the legal ruling regarding the implementation of Allah's law, and that faith is not complete without it, and he knows the punishment for disobedience to Allah, and when he conceives the torment of Hell and what Allah has prepared for the sinners therein, his heart trembles, and his skin shivers when he remembers all this and brings its image to mind. He rushes to work towards implementing Allah's law, seeking the pleasure of Allah, fleeing from His anger and punishment, thus his motivation to work for the implementation of Allah's law becomes strong. This work becomes accompanied by it, and he finds himself only in sessions of dialogue, discussion, and calling to Allah, and to the implementation of His law, hastening towards it, coming forward and not turning back, rejoicing in what Allah has prepared for His working servants of high ranks and greater pleasure from Allah.

- Connecting reality with information
- Connecting information with reality

1-     Reason
2-     Contemplation
3-     Repentance
4-     Guidance

5-      Faith
6-      Intellectual distinction
7-      Response

All of these meanings are unified by a common concept, which is presence of mind, that is, the mental image of the intended matter in those verses, and presence of mind necessitates the connection of the recalled concept to its motivator and the accompanying inclination.
The term "remembrance" (tadhakkur) has been used in the Qur'an in two different ways:
First: When it mentions a cosmic matter, or a tangible reality, it follows it with one of the derivatives of remembrance, which means linking the tangible matter to previous information, relying on faith.
Second: When it speaks about the Qur'an, or about an idea, or about a legal ruling, it follows it with remembrance, which means linking the information or idea to its reality, relying on faith.
Let us clarify this with some examples of each of these two methods:
First: The occurrence of the term remembrance with the linking of reality to information
1-  Allah, Exalted is He, said: “Did We not grant you a long enough life so that whoever would remember could remember, and the warner came to you?” In this verse, Allah establishes a proof against the disbelievers by giving them a sufficient lifespan for remembrance, a lifespan that includes things, events, and relationships, as it necessitates linking them to the previous information in a person, and determining the perspective towards them. Despite this, He sent them a warner who provides them with the correct intellectual foundations to build concepts about things.
2-  Allah, Exalted is He, said: “And it is He who made the night and the day succeeding each other for whoever desires to remember or desires gratitude” (Al-Furqan/62). In this verse, Allah informs us of His making the night and day alternate with their events, things, and relationships, and that in this is a reminder for whoever desires. The Muslim connects what he sees and observes with the information he possesses, building that on faith, to form a new concept for everything he has seen, witnessed, and experienced during the night or day.

And We have certainly distributed it among them that they might remember, but most of the people refused except for disbelief” (Al-Furqan/48-52). The distribution of rain and watering, and the observation of people witnessing that, necessitates remembrance, that is, contemplation and linking reality to the related information based on the intellectual foundation.
4-  Allah, Exalted is He, said: “O Children of Adam, We have certainly sent down to you clothing to conceal your private parts and as adornment; and the clothing of righteousness—that is best. That is from the signs of Allah that perhaps they will remember” (Al-A'raf/26). Contemplation of clothing and reflection upon it and linking it to previous information is based on the intellectual foundation.

The methods of Islam in establishing the correct understanding of the concept:
Islam has employed many diverse methods to establish the correct understanding of the concept it seeks to convey and provide to the Muslim. Among these methods:
1- The use of examples: The Noble Qur'an and the pure Sunnah have frequently employed examples to depict the reality being discussed in a manner that is as close as possible to the senses, in order to establish the concept, blend it with its motivation, and shape the accompanying inclination. Allah, Exalted is He, said: “And We have certainly presented for the people in this Qur'an every kind of example that perhaps they will remember” {39:27}, and He, Exalted is He, said: “And Allah presents examples for the people that perhaps they will remember” {14:25}.
Among the examples of parables in the Noble Qur'an is His saying: “And Allah sets forth the example of a good word like a good tree, whose root is firm and its branches are in the sky...” (Ibrahim). Thus, the Exalted One depicted the good word as a good tree with a firm root in the ground, its branches extending into the sky, so that the concept of the good word is associated in the mind of the Muslim with a pleasing image that encourages its possessor to seek it and adhere to it.
One example of the use of parables in the Sunnah is his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "The example of the one who upholds the limits of Allah and the one who transgresses them is like a group of people who cast lots on a ship. Some of them got the upper deck, and some of them got the lower deck. Those who were in the lower deck, when they wanted water, would pass by those in the upper deck..."


[Page 14]

They said: "If we were to make a hole in our share, we would not harm those above us. If they leave them alone, they will perish, and they will all perish together. But if they take them by the hand, they will be saved, and they will all be saved." In this hadith, there is a representation of the community that operates within the limits set by Allah, and the community that exists within the boundaries of Allah, in order to link the concept to an image that is associated with it in the mind, strengthening the required concept, which is enjoining what is good and forbidding what is wrong, and ruling by what Allah has revealed.
2- Artistic imagery: as His Exalted saying: “And do not backbite one another. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother while he is dead? You would detest it.” In this imagery, there is an unmistakable aversion to the sin intended here, and the Qur'an, with such imagery, makes the legal ruling beloved to the Muslim or repels him from the disobedience of Allah, Exalted is He.
3- Stories: Allah, Exalted is He, said: "And thus We relate to you from the news of the towns what We strengthen thereby your heart," and He, Exalted is He, said: "Indeed, in their stories is a lesson for those of understanding." The Noble Qur'an employs the method of narrating the stories of the ancients, which contain lessons that contribute to the strengthening of the heart of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and the bearers of the message after him.
4- The Environment: Islam, in its rulings, legislations, and treatments, aims to create a sound environment that influences both the individual and the community towards the optimal shaping of the psychology. Thus, if an individual's piety falls short of elevating him or of deterring him from actions contrary to Islam, then public opinion serves as a deterrent for him. The bad appears in society like a black spot on the skin of a white bull, which stirs within the individual a sense of deficiency and criticism from others. Consequently, the surrounding environment assists the individual in shaping his psychology in the optimal and elevating manner, helping him avoid the pitfalls of the self. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The believer is a mirror to his brother," so a Muslim sees through the eyes of his brother the faults and violations within himself, and he strives to correct and overcome them.
5- Encouragement and Warning: The Noble Qur'an employs the methods of encouragement and warning in most of its rulings, which stirs within the Muslim's soul a longing for what his heart desires and fear of what it does not desire. Indeed, He, Exalted is He, has encouraged the paradise and what it contains of everlasting bliss, and has warned against the fire and what it contains of painful torment. This makes the motivation for piety and adherence to the commands of Allah, Exalted is He, and avoidance of His prohibitions intrinsic. Thus, one refrains from unlawful earnings, awaiting what Allah has prepared for him in paradise, and abstains from unlawful gaze and forbidden sexual gratification, yearning for the houris in paradise. He draws closer to Allah, Exalted is He, fleeing from falling into sins and escaping from entering the fire. There are many examples in the Noble Qur'an and the pure Sunnah regarding encouragement and warning; I will mention here just one example.
Allah, Exalted is He, said: {And do not think that Allah is unaware of what the wrongdoers do. Fear
Fear is a manifestation of the instinct for survival, and it is inevitably present in humans because it is part of their constitution, existing within them by nature. However, like other manifestations of the survival instinct such as dominance, defense, compassion, and others, as well as other instincts like religiosity and procreation, it only appears in the presence of a factor that provokes it. If this provoking factor does not exist, fear does not manifest at all. The factor that provokes fear is the same as that which provokes any instinct, and it can be either a tangible material thing or a thought connected to it or related to it. However, this material thing or thought must be something that is perceived as frightening or something that is felt emotionally to be frightening. If this perception or feeling does not occur, fear does not arise because the instinct does not activate its energy or become agitated unless the thing is associated with feelings of fear through perception or instinctual differentiation. Thus, it only occurs with the presence of something that provokes it, even if it is innate and created with the creation of man.
Fear is a serious problem that the degraded peoples and weak nations suffer from, leading to humiliation and backwardness. When fear dominates a person, it deprives them of the joy of living, robs them of the noblest qualities, causes mental confusion and an inability to judge things, and paralyzes their memory and capacity for differentiation.

7- The formulation of Islam regarding a Muslim's perspective on death elevates him to higher matters without fear of what he may encounter in pursuit of them. If the most dangerous thing threatening a person is death, and this knot has been resolved for him, he will no longer fear anything in his journey towards higher matters and the pleasure of Allah, the Exalted, and he will fulfill what Allah, the Exalted, has commanded him without fear, and he will refrain from what Allah, the Exalted, has prohibited him without fear of anyone else.
