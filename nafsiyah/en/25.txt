2- Finding contentment that Allah is the One who has decreed death upon His servants, “He who created death and life” {Al-Mulk/2}, and being pleased with this conviction as a means of drawing closer to Allah, the Exalted, and submitting to what Allah, the Most High, has willed.
3- Islam has made death a motivation for doing good, for when the thought of death crosses the mind of a Muslim, and it is essential that it always does; he remembers what comes after death, from terrors and accountability, which serves as a motivation for him to increase his closeness to Allah, the Exalted, in order to avoid the dangers that follow death, “He who created death and life to test you which of you is best in deed” {Al-Mulk/2}.
4- Islam has linked the time of death to the decree of Allah, the Exalted. Allah, the Most High, says: “We have decreed death among you, and We are not to be outstripped in replacing you with others like you and creating you in that which you do not know” {Al-Waqi‘ah/60-61}.
5- Islam considers death an obstacle preventing a Muslim from entering Paradise; the Muslim sees death as a barrier to entering Paradise, thus he hastens towards it in the places where Allah, the Exalted, has commanded, such as in the contexts of fighting in the way of Allah. It has been reported from one of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with him) that he said: “If I live until I eat these dates of mine, it will be a long life.” He then threw away what he had of dates and fought them until he was killed. This was after his conversation with the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) when the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) gave him the glad tidings that he was among the people of Paradise. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Whoever recites Ayat al-Kursi after every prayer, nothing prevents him from entering Paradise except death.”
6- Islam has linked the arrival of death to the end of the lifespan that Allah, the Exalted, has decreed for the servant. Allah, the Most High, says: “And when their time comes, they will not remain behind an hour, nor will they precede it.” It seeks to instill in the hearts of the believers that nothing leads to death except when a person's time has ended, so a person should not fear his enemy, as he is prepared to meet him without fearing death, for his death has a set time, which neither the encounter with the enemy brings closer nor does cowardice and fear of fighting push it away.
7- The formulation of Islam regarding a Muslim's perspective on death elevates him to higher matters without fear of what he may encounter in pursuit of them. If the most dangerous thing threatening a person is death, and this knot has been resolved for him, he will no longer fear anything in his journey towards higher matters and the pleasure of Allah, the Exalted, and he will fulfill what Allah, the Exalted, has commanded him without fear, and he will refrain from what Allah, the Exalted, has prohibited him without fear of anyone else.
