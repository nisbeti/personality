With the human realization that everything that occurs in this universe is by the command of Allah, His power, will, and management, he – that is, the human – seeks to understand how this matter relates to him by reflecting on the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "Amazing is the affair of the believer; all of his affair is good for him. If he is afflicted with ease, he is grateful, and that is good for him; and if he is afflicted with hardship, he is patient, and that is good for him. And this is not the case except for the believer." Thus, his perspective on what happens in this universe transforms into seeing it as good for him, leading him to be content and reassured, distancing himself from a futile outlook, and linking everything that happens to the will of Allah and His wise management. He praises Allah for what pleases him with acceptance, and he is patient and praises Allah for what does not please him, being content with it and enduring it, and he anticipates the consequences and reward from Allah, saying: "O Allah, reward me in my affliction and replace it with something better." Therefore, reassurance remains with him in all his states, and this is only for the believer, as the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, and the reason for this is faith, as indicated by the meaning of the text.

Allah, Exalted is He, said: "And whoever believes in Allah, He will guide his heart." This verse guarantees guidance of the heart for those who believe in Allah, and the Noble Qur'an has used the term "heart" in many places to express feelings and emotions, as it has also used it to describe the psychological impact resulting from thoughts and concepts. He said about the disbelievers: "They have hearts with which they do not understand." It is known that understanding, which is comprehension, resides in the intellect, but due to what occurs in the heart of inner reassurance and psychological contentment accompanying understanding, and the psychological stability that accompanies the idea being contemplated by the human. And since understanding is the cause of the heart's reassurance and its contentment, the Noble Qur'an has employed a metaphorical expression, where it attributed the effect, which is the heart and its reassurance, to the cause, which is understanding, contemplation, and intellect.

The guidance of the heart here – which pertains to feelings and emotions – is intended to shape those feelings and emotions according to what Allah, Exalted is He, desires. This is because faith necessitates following, obedience, acceptance, and submission to what has come from Allah. What the Sharia has brought ensures the shaping of those feelings and emotions in a specific manner that aligns with the ideas and concepts of the Sharia. The commands and prohibitions of Allah, along with their details, focus on that shaping, transforming feelings and emotions into inclinations. The transformation into inclinations necessitates regulating their arousal with the related concepts, allowing the human to elevate his instincts and organic needs to the highest level, distancing himself from the danger of degradation to the level of animals. If he sees something that causes fear, that vision will not be a cause for his fear unless it aligns with his concepts about that thing. If it involves disobedience, then it deserves fear, as it entails the anger of Allah and His wrath and punishment. If it is fear for sustenance, it will not affect him, due to his belief that his provision is decreed by Allah, and he is reassured about that, so no vein will pulse with fear or dread, and so on.
