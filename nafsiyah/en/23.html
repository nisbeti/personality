Fear

Fear is a manifestation of the instinct for survival, and it is inevitably present in humans because it is part of their constitution, existing within them by nature. However, like other manifestations of the survival instinct such as dominance, defense, compassion, and others, as well as other instincts like religiosity and procreation, it only appears in the presence of a factor that provokes it. If this provoking factor does not exist, fear does not manifest at all. The factor that provokes fear is the same as that which provokes any instinct, and it can be either a tangible material thing or a thought connected to it or related to it. However, this material thing or thought must be something that is perceived as frightening or something that is felt emotionally to be frightening. If this perception or feeling does not occur, fear does not arise because the instinct does not activate its energy or become agitated unless the thing is associated with feelings of fear through perception or instinctual differentiation. Thus, it only occurs with the presence of something that provokes it, even if it is innate and created with the creation of man.

Fear is a serious problem that the degraded peoples and weak nations suffer from, leading to humiliation and backwardness. When fear dominates a person, it deprives them of the joy of living, robs them of the noblest qualities, causes mental confusion and an inability to judge things, and paralyzes their memory and capacity for differentiation.

The most dangerous type of fear is the fear of illusions and phantoms. This occurs only among those with weak minds, either because their mental development is not yet complete, like children, or due to a lack of sufficient information to connect with reality, such as the ignorant and those who lack information due to their life in society, like most women, or due to an innate weakness in their brains, such as the feeble-minded and the insane, and those similar to them. For these individuals, fear can be treated either by delving deeply into discussions with them and clarifying things for their understanding, or by providing them with ideas connected to what they fear, provided that these ideas have a tangible reality for them. Through this treatment, they can rid themselves of the grip of fear, either by eliminating it or by gradually alleviating it until the remnants of its concepts in their depths are uprooted.

There is a type of fear that is less dangerous than the fear of illusions, which is the fear resulting from an incorrect estimation of things. This occurs when a person sees something that may be frightening or may not be frightening. For instance, they might see a sleeping dog and think it is a rabid dog because they previously saw a similar rabid dog, causing them to fear passing by and to run away from it. If they were to verify, they would see that it is a tame dog that poses no threat, as it is sleeping and unaware of their passing. They might see a lion in a cage and fear approaching the cage lest the lion come out to them, and if the lion roars, their fear increases, prompting them to flee, thinking it might escape from the cage, and so on. Most misjudgments occur regarding intangible matters, such as writing an article or giving a speech in a place, or conversing with a ruler or discussing with a person of influence, and similar situations. The failure to correctly estimate things causes fear, leading them to refrain from writing, speaking, or discussing out of fear of harm.
