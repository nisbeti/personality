Secondly: The occurrence of the term "remembrance" with the connection of information to reality:
1- Allah, Exalted is He, said: “A Book which We have sent down to you, blessed, that they may reflect upon its verses and that those of understanding may be reminded” {Sad/29}. In this noble verse, the wisdom behind the revelation of the Qur'an is to reflect upon its verses in a way that leads to remembrance, which is the act of bringing to mind, meaning to connect what is in it to the reality it discusses. This implies that one should study the Qur'an verse by verse, linking each verse to its context, while relying on the correct foundations derived from the intellectual framework.
2- Allah, Exalted is He, said: “A surah We have sent down and made obligatory, and in it We have sent down clear verses so that you may remember” {al-Nur: 1}. What has been revealed in Surah al-Nur is a collection of legal rulings related to the social system, along with some other ideas, and the intended fruit of this is remembrance, that is, recalling the legal ruling along with the reality it was revealed to address, formulating the motivations related to the actions that the legal ruling was revealed to address, and shaping the inclination towards it. The result is aversion to adultery, false accusation, and forbidden gaze,...
3- Allah, Exalted is He, said: “O you who have believed, do not enter houses other than your own houses until you seek permission and greet their inhabitants. That is best for you, that perhaps you will be reminded” {An-Nur/27}. The term "reminded" appears here after a legal ruling, which necessitates the presence of awareness that combines the ruling with the motivation and the formulation of the accompanying inclination in terms of acceptance and submission.
The methods of Islam in establishing the correct understanding of the concept:
Islam has employed many diverse methods to establish the correct understanding of the concept it seeks to convey and provide to the Muslim. Among these methods:
1- The use of examples: The Noble Qur'an and the pure Sunnah have frequently employed examples to depict the reality being discussed in a manner that is as close as possible to the senses, in order to establish the concept, blend it with its motivation, and shape the accompanying inclination. Allah, Exalted is He, said: “And We have certainly presented for the people in this Qur'an every kind of example that perhaps they will remember” {39:27}, and He, Exalted is He, said: “And Allah presents examples for the people that perhaps they will remember” {14:25}.
Among the examples of parables in the Noble Qur'an is His saying: “And Allah sets forth the example of a good word like a good tree, whose root is firm and its branches are in the sky...” (Ibrahim). Thus, the Exalted One depicted the good word as a good tree with a firm root in the ground, its branches extending into the sky, so that the concept of the good word is associated in the mind of the Muslim with a pleasing image that encourages its possessor to seek it and adhere to it.
One example of the use of parables in the Sunnah is his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "The example of the one who upholds the limits of Allah and the one who transgresses them is like a group of people who cast lots on a ship. Some of them got the upper deck, and some of them got the lower deck. Those who were in the lower deck, when they wanted water, would pass by those in the upper deck..."
