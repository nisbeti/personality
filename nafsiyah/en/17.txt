5- Acts of worship: Islam has made acts of worship an accessible means to maintain the connection between the servant and his Lord. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said in the hadith he narrates from his Lord: "My servant does not draw near to Me with anything more beloved to Me than what I have made obligatory upon him, and My servant continues to draw near to Me with voluntary acts until I love him. When I love him, I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he strikes, and his foot with which he walks. If he asks Me, I will give him, and if he seeks refuge with Me, I will grant him refuge."
6- Supplication: Islam has also made supplication a strong link between the Muslim and his Creator, which keeps the heart's connection to Allah (Exalted is He) constant. Thus, the measures remain alert within the individual, and he hardly forgets or becomes heedless. Allah (Exalted is He) says: "And your Lord says, 'Call upon Me; I will respond to you. Indeed, those who disdain My worship will enter Hell, disgraced.'" And He says: "And when My servants ask you concerning Me, indeed I am near. I respond to the invocation of the supplicant when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me that they may be guided."

The most important ideas that contribute to shaping the psychology in a specific manner:
Just as the overarching idea resolves the major dilemma for a person, Islam has brought forth a set of concepts that resolve the subsidiary dilemmas for him. Upon examining these concepts, we find that they are:
1- Basic beliefs:
- Belief in Allah (Exalted is He) and His attributes
- Belief that the Qur'an is the word of Allah
- Belief that Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) is the Messenger of Allah
- Belief in the Last Day
2- Secondary beliefs:
- Belief in divine decree (qadar)
- Belief in destiny and divine decree
- Belief that the end of life is the only reason for death
- Belief that provision is from Allah
- The belief in reliance (tawakkul)
- The belief in guidance and misguidance
- The belief in victory
3- Standards:
- Standard of good and evil
- Standard of benefit and harm
- Standard of ideas
- Standard of behavior (lawful and unlawful)
