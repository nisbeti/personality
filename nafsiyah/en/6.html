And a human being has a vital energy that drives him towards satisfaction, which consists of organic needs and instincts, and human behavior; that is, the actions he performs are to satisfy those organic needs and instincts. These drives for satisfaction, if they are linked to his intellectual foundation and the concepts derived from it, become inclinations and shape the psychology of the individual.

In his behavior, he is driven to satisfy an innate need or a manifestation of instincts within him, and his behavior for satisfaction is linked to a specific concept about this satisfaction. This drive to engage in behavior becomes regulated by the related concept, and in addition to that, the drive is accompanied by a certain feeling towards the behavior that satisfies, making the concept influential in the individual from two aspects:

- The intellectual aspect: that is, it determines the ruling on this satisfaction, whether it is satisfied in this way or in another, and it defines his stance towards that event and his perspective on that thing. If this occurs, he develops a mentality characterized by the concepts he holds and the intellectual foundation from which those concepts emerged.

- The psychological aspect: that is, it determines the type of feeling accompanying the satisfaction, whether it is contentment or anger, love or hatred, longing for it or aversion from it, and so on. If this occurs, the feelings accompanying the drives and the behaviors resulting from them are formulated, transforming into inclinations, and he develops a psychology characterized by the concepts that contributed to the formulation of feelings and their transformation into inclinations.

If the concepts that contribute to the formulation of feelings and their transformation into inclinations align with the concepts that determine the type of behavior, the individual acquires a distinctive personality defined by the intellectual foundation from which those controlling concepts in both the intellectual and psychological aspects emerged, thus coloring his personality. If the concepts are Islamic and stem from Islamic belief, the personality becomes Islamic; if the concepts are capitalist and stem from capitalist belief, the personality becomes capitalist. If this does not occur for a specific individual, his personality may be chaotic or contradictory.

If this happens, the belief becomes for him an intellectual leadership, meaning it provides its holder with criteria that guide his judgments about things, his drives towards them, and his inclinations towards them in a specific direction necessitated by that belief, thus becoming a leadership. And since specific ideas govern this, and those ideas stem from an intellectual foundation which is the belief, it becomes intellectual, and hence the term (intellectual leadership) arises.

- Ideas: the plural of idea, and an idea is a judgment on reality, and it is called an idea because it is the product of a thinking process, after transferring reality through the senses to the brain and linking it with previous information about that reality. The product of this linking process is a judgment on that reality, which is the idea. Thus, our statement: "There is no deity but Allah," is an idea because it is a judgment on the perceived reality from the perspective of its creation.
